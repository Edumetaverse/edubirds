THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE SCENE

Watch, change, explain.

01 / FOUR FORCES

Air and gravity act together

Lift pushes up, weight pulls down, thrust moves a bird forward and drag resists that motion. Flapping wings push air down and back, helping create lift and thrust.

02 / FLAP OR GLIDE

One bird, two ways to fly

In a glide, the wings stay open and no new thrust comes from wingbeats. During flapping, the moving wings help the bird move forward as well as stay aloft.

03 / FAIR TEST

Change one thing

Keep airspeed and wing spread steady. Compare flap, glide and alternate. Watch the net force, then watch the bird’s rise or sink speed. Can it still be rising when the force turns downward?

N means newtons, a unit for measuring force. This is a teaching model of vertical flight. Airflow lift uses NASA’s lift equation and a published 4.6 kg, 45 N, 0.65 m², 16 m/s golden eagle reference only to set its scale. The lift coefficient stays fixed. Each beat per second adds an assumed 5 N of average lift while flapping; this is an illustrative setting, not a measurement for all birds. Vertical air resistance opposes the bird’s rise or sink speed. The displayed net vertical force is total lift minus weight plus that resistance, and acceleration follows force divided by mass. Height is bounded between 0 and 60 m for the experiment, and the 3D scene compresses that range. Forward thrust and forward drag arrows are qualitative; the airspeed control holds forward speed fixed. Real birds continuously change wing shape, speed and direction.
