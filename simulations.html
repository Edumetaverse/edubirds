THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE SCENE

Watch, change, explain.

01 / FOUR FORCES

Air and gravity act together

Lift pushes up, weight pulls down, thrust moves a bird forward and drag resists that motion. Flapping wings push air down and back, helping create lift and thrust.

02 / FLAP OR GLIDE

One bird, two ways to fly

In a glide, the wings stay open and no new thrust comes from wingbeats. During flapping, the moving wings help the bird move forward as well as stay aloft.

03 / FAIR TEST

Change one thing

Hold wing spread steady and compare slow and fast air. Then keep airspeed steady and change wing spread. Which change affects the lift arrow?

N means newtons, a unit for measuring force. The numeric airflow-lift example uses NASA’s lift equation and a single published golden eagle reference (4.6 kg, 45 N, 0.65 m² wing area, 16 m/s modelled cruise speed) to set its scale. It is a general explanation of bird-flight forces, not a model of every species. The lift coefficient stays fixed, wing spread scales exposed area, and the 3D position is schematic. Wingbeats add lift and thrust that the numeric airflow estimate does not include; thrust and drag arrows are qualitative. Real birds continually change speed and wing shape.
