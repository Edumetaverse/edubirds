EDUAVIARY / TEACHER GUIDE

Bird detective: who belongs at the wetland?

A 25-minute investigation for NSW Stage 2–3 students. Students compare a white-faced heron and rainbow lorikeet, choose evidence for a habitat claim, then test one variable in the flight lab or observe a bird outdoors.

Open student mission ↗
Learning intention

Use observations to explain how a bird feature can help it find food in a particular habitat.

Success criteria

I can compare two birds, select a relevant feature, and explain the link between the feature, habitat and food.

Lesson sequence

  1. Engage · 3 min

    Ask: “Could any bird feed equally well in shallow water?” Invite a prediction without confirming an answer.

  2. Observe and compare · 7 min

    Open the bird mission. Students compare heron and lorikeet beaks, feet, food and habitat. Then open the linked BirdLife Australia pages to check real photographs and the lorikeet call.

  3. Claim and evidence · 6 min

    Students choose the wetland bird and a useful feature, then write a sentence explaining how that feature helps it get food. Use the feedback to revise. The app checks the selected bird and feature; a teacher or peer checks the written reasoning.

  4. Investigate · 6 min

    In the 3D flight lab, students predict and run two six-second trials, changing one control. They compare height change and note that the model uses assumed values.

  5. Reflect · 3 min

    Ask students what they learned from a model and what they need to check by observing real birds. Students can download their response and field notes as a text file using “Save my response”.

Adjust for each stage

Stage 2

Provide a sentence frame: “The ___ has ___. This helps it ___ in ___.” Focus on describing a fair comparison and one change at a time in the flight lab.

Stage 3

Ask students to justify their choice using two observations, compare flight trial results, and distinguish a model assumption from measured evidence. Extend to a food web: what could change if wetland prey declined?

Curriculum connections

These are suggested teaching connections, not a formal mapping. Check your school’s adopted syllabus and program. The NSW Science and Technology K–6 (2024) outcomes include Stage 2 questioning for fair tests and interpreting data (ST2-PQU-01, ST2-DAT-01). NSW planning guidance describes Stage 3 work on adaptations, food webs and evidence-based explanations. The 2024 syllabus begins implementation from 2027; schools may prepare or adopt it earlier.

Questions and checks

  • Does the student link a feature to a useful action in the habitat, rather than simply naming a feature?
  • Can the student tell a generated illustration from a real photograph?
  • Can the student explain which flight values are measured reference data and which are teaching assumptions?
  • Does the student avoid saying a bird chose to grow its adaptation? Species change over generations.

The field journal saves on the current device only. The downloaded response is a plain-text file that the student can give to a teacher. No classroom account or server submission is required.
